Artist CV · San Francisco

jawno okhiulu

freedom singer, guitar boy, dj get free

Practice

My artistic practice is participatory song, incorporating my family heritage of call and response and other technologies within the Black musical tradition to build powerful resonance between people and to get us moving toward a world where we're better toward each other and the planet we call home.

I'm a singer-songwriter of African American and Nigerian heritage, working in the Black choral and griot storytelling traditions. I make music with people, not only for them, and I organize the conditions for other artists to do the same.

Now

Socially engaged practice

  • Monthly

    Noe Valley Night Market

    Regular performer at the monthly night market in San Francisco, singing soul covers and originals.

  • 2025–present

    Founder, Each Artist Cultural Hub (EACH)

    An artist-led network for Bay Area artists. EACH hosts monthly artist gatherings for connection, inspiration, and resource sharing, and has convened 200+ artists with partners including SOMArts, Artist Space Trust, and Calle 24. I'm designing the Community Arts Navigators pilot, a peer model inspired by community health workers in which resourced artists help fellow artists and neighbors find space, resources, and belonging.

  • 2026

    Co-lead, Artists Live Here

    A recurring evening at SOMArts for artists to gather and build collective power, co-led with artist Shrey Purohit. The next edition returns in early 2027.

  • 2025

    Dalai Lama Global Fellow

    Fellowship supporting the early development of EACH.

  • 2022–present

    Guest lecturer, Stanford University

    Annual session in EARTHSYS 194E: Introduction to Environmental Justice, connecting land ethics, food sovereignty, and personal narrative with community storytelling and healing practice.

Before

Performance, community, teaching

  • 2023 & 2024

    Garden After Dark

    Dancer, vocalist, and choreographic collaborator with The Chocolate Heads Movement Band, directed by Aleta Hayes. Stanford, CA.

  • From 2022

    Black Earth Farms

    Designed and taught the Black Land Ethic pedagogy and workshop; supported development of the California Black Farmer Resource Hub.

  • Summers from 2022

    [HS]2, Carbondale, CO

    Designed and taught a garden, kitchen, and classroom course on cultivating food justice for high school students.

  • 2017–2021

    Stanford Talisman A Cappella

    Bass vocalist, then director (2020–21). Led 22 singers in music centering Black and Indigenous narratives, raised $10K+ for Bay Area community organizations including Destiny Arts, and produced a virtual alumni choir of “Lift Every Voice and Sing” with 80+ voices during the pandemic. Toured as a vocalist to India and Hawaii.
